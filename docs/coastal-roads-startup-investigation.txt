COASTAL ROADS, FOUNDATIONS AND STARTUP — 12 October 2026

Implemented foundation correction
- Previous code sampled WorldLayout's pre-render datum. Actual terrain is
  2.5 m lower; subtracting only 1–2 m could leave foundations hovering.
- BridgeStructurePlan now samples the rendered terrain datum over the full
  rotated support footprint, including both offset suspension legs.
- Foundations continue five metres below the lowest sampled ground and the
  bounding terrain grid vertices for all current LODs (25/50/100/200 m).
  This conservative lower bound keeps the bottom buried during LOD changes.
- Bank piers, approach piers, tower legs, landfall blocks and anchor blocks
  use these bases. Approach endpoints now meet the correct terrain datum.
- Full family/three-seed placement tests pass; a sloping-ground regression
  also checks footprint and coarse-LOD burial. No constant-length legs.

Road/settlement investigation (seed 42, 40 km)
211 houses, 3,308 two-point road strips, 1,407 endpoint-connected components,
largest component only seven nodes, maximum endpoint grade approximately 60%.
34 houses occupy a different sampled land component from their assigned port.
The current planner scans coastline within 850 m of each port. It offsets
roads inland by 145/195 m, then joins adjacent samples only when <55 m apart
and <5 m different in height. This is neither terrain routing nor a graph of
destinations/junctions. Five metres rise over a short segment can be very steep.
It has no shared graph for port exits, bridge ends, villages or house access.
Nearby unrelated islands can receive houses because straight-line proximity
is used without land-component ownership. Not every island is populated today,
but there is no deliberate inhabited/wild island policy.
Forest exclusion is building footprint +4 m, narrow drive strips and road
strips. This clears trunk roots, not a garden, usable plot, village frontage
or sightline towards the sea; trees 10–14 m tall can still hide small houses.

Recommended next road pass (not implemented here)
1. Stable inhabited/wild designation by land component, available buildable
   area, port access and a sparse seeded settlement budget. Bridges alone
   must not populate every stepping-stone island.
2. A small persistent data graph: ports, accepted bridge landfalls, villages
   and junctions. Sparse regional connections, not house-by-house world work.
3. Deterministic terrain-aware routes between those anchors; explicit grade,
   width, water and slope constraints. Local earthworks/foundations where
   needed; do not drape a road ribbon across arbitrarily steep terrain.
4. Place occupied plots and their entrances from the road graph. Define
   gardens, driveways, open village space and selective coastal views before
   vegetation is sampled. Preserve wild woodland between settlements.
5. Generate/cache regional detail with fixed region ownership/borders so
   approaching in a different order cannot change roads or settlements.
   Walkable collision, props and detailed plots only near actual observers.
   Cheap distant representations; movement-triggered queues with budgets.

Startup changes implemented now
- Immutable geography from the captain/home-port picker is retained, not
  cleared immediately when entering its world.
- Terrain raster bake uses up to four private row workers; results concatenate
  in original row order before the unchanged redistance/checksum stages.
- Disposable on-disk caches for terrain raster, seeded port records and forest
  coverage. Keys include generator/config identity or layout identity and
  relevant inputs. Payload SHA-256 detects damage; no Object deserialization.
  Port records reconstruct fresh objects so development captain mutations do
  not leak into later loads. Terrain and port algorithms remain unchanged.
- Seamarks plan on a worker instead of blocking world setup; loaded/placed
  near the observer as before. Worker and synchronous output parity checked.
- Settlement mesh/road chunks load within 2.8 km and unload beyond 3.3 km.
  Queues update after 128 m movement; collider checks visit loaded chunks.
  Previously all settlement chunks were eventually built and retained.
