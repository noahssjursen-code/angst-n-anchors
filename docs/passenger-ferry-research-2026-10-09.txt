Bergen-inspired fast passenger ferry and terminal
Research checkpoint: 9 October 2026

Noah requested a passenger pier/terminal and high-speed catamaran, with Skyss
and Norled boats around Bergen as references. This is research and a build
brief, not a completed vessel or tested passenger service. Multiplayer remains
out of scope. Existing accepted ocean and player locomotion stay unchanged.

Reference choice

Use the Brødrene Aa / Norled Fjordbris family as the principal visual reference.
Builder data: 36.7 x 10.8 m, 240 passengers, carbon-sandwich/vinylester structure,
35-knot service speed and Servogear controllable-pitch propellers. [1]
Servogear lists four 900 kW engines and 1,450 mm propellers; its table says
38 knots while the description says 35-knot service. Treat 35 as the reference
cruise, not a guaranteed game speed or a reason to impose a velocity cap. [2]
The two sources differ on delivery year (2013/2014); this does not affect the
game design. Gross tonnage on the builder page is NOT displacement mass.

Useful alternatives: Fjordlys is a smaller 27 x 8.8 m, 180-passenger, 30-knot
Norled catamaran [3]. Oma confirms that Vingtor, Njord and Frøya were built for
Norled's Bergen/Sogn services, with 290/290/190 passengers respectively [4].
Skyss lists both local Bergen commuter services and longer regional routes [5].
These are distinct operational references; do not call every Norled boat a
current Bergen commuter boat or apply one vessel's machinery to another.

Visual inspection completed

Actually viewed the builder's Fjordbris exterior, wheelhouse, saloon and control
photos, plus Oma's Bergen harbour photo. Observed a long dark window band, low
white superstructure, dark twin hulls and a raised red bridge. The saloon has
proper seat rows, clear aisles, large windows, ceiling panels and downlights;
the bridge uses seated controls and wraparound glazing. These are references
for original modular artwork, not game textures or permission to redistribute
the photographs. Source images remain outside the repository.

Read and rendered the relevant Skyss/Rambøll 2021 terminal study pages [6].
Printed p31 documents the terminal building and separate waiting shelter at
Strandkaien. Printed pp25 and 41 describe bow boarding with side support, and
the wide bow gangway's passenger-flow advantages. Figures 28-31 distinguish
existing quays from proposed angled floating piers and show alongside regional
boats as well. The floating-pier layouts are study proposals, not evidence of
what was built. Use these as design precedents, not current construction claims.

Recommended first game vessel: working title Coastal Express 240

Original editable 36.5 x 11 m catamaran, rounded to the builder lattice. Target
roughly 200-240 usable passenger places; settle the exact count after a measured
interior layout and player clearance review. Never grant 240 merely from the
name. Aiming for the reference's 35-knot cruise requires measured displacement,
resistance and machinery tuning with empty and occupied loads.

The silhouette should be recognizable before adding fittings: two slender
demihulls with an open tunnel; a raised connecting deck; long glazed passenger
saloon; an upper forward saloon/bridge mass with a restrained swept front; a
clear bow landing platform and independently moving ramp. Include a side
boarding door so an alongside terminal remains useful too. Avoid turning the
existing freighter into a ferry by attaching a second hull or stretching cabins.

Build in Blender as reusable components: hull assembly with separately identified
demihulls/deck; saloon wall/window bays and end sections; roof sections; angled
bridge glazing; bow ramp, hinges and guardrails; side door; seat rows; luggage
rack; accessible toilet enclosure; small kiosk; ventilation and machinery covers.
Reuse approved railings, navigation lights, bitts, liferafts, helm instruments
and compatible stairs. Each movable part retains a real pivot and walk collision.
Keep separate Blender sources, exported assets, paint regions and mounting data.

Finish: white marine paint/composite, dark hull finish, rubber rubbing strakes,
metal rails, deliberate glass tint, textured upholstery and nonslip flooring.
Use the approved SurfaceMaterialLibrary wherever suitable. Keep wear around
boarding thresholds, machinery access and waterlines; do not cover a composite
passenger ferry in freighter-style rust. Livery remains recolourable.

Recommended terminal kit

First model one compact terminal with a real waiting room, desk, WC/service
space, glazed waterfront facade, canopy, seating, information board and a clear
pedestrian path to the gate. Initial game layout allowance: approximately
18 x 10 m building plus its forecourt, not a surveyed Strandkaien replica.
Make a shelter-only rural variant from the same kit later.

One dedicated passenger berth with a broad bow landing, fenders, side support,
bollards, edge protection, ladder, lifering and practical lighting. Author a
roughly 45 x 15 m vessel clearance envelope for the proposed 36.5 x 11 m ferry;
this is an initial game allowance to verify, not an engineering standard.
Reserve room for backing out and turning. Boarding path and mooring lines must
not cross. Separate the public approach from cargo machinery and truck routes.
An alongside layout is a second variant using the same terminal and fittings.

Use a named boarding interface: terminal landing pose, boarding direction,
allowed vessel envelope and landing-height range; ship ramp/door sockets and
their clearance. Bow and alongside berths need explicit approach poses. Do not
pretend bow-in arrival is a 90-degree rotation of the current long-side mooring
logic. A fixed pier with a ship-mounted moving ramp is sufficient for the first
working slice. Floating pontoons/charging infrastructure are later options.

Current project findings (read-only inspection)

- scripts/ship/vessels/passenger_catamaran.gd is a legacy 45 x 16 m physical
  hull. _build_hull_visual currently creates an empty anchor. Its 2.4 MN thrust
  multiplied by 19.5 m/s / 0.72 implies about 65 MW. Do not use this as a tuned
  passenger-vessel baseline. It does demonstrate separated demihull buoyancy
  and collision slices, which can inform the current imported pipeline.
- ImportedHullCatalog has no imported catamaran. ImportedDraftVessel.configure
  builds one aggregate station set, invokes the monohull system assembly, and
  wraps imported hull vertices in one convex collision. For a catamaran that
  would fill the tunnel. Use explicit demihull stations and separate collision
  shapes, with valid equilibrium draft, roll response and mass distribution.
- MarineEngineCatalog has one machinery mount per hull; ShipDriveVisual has
  one propeller/rudder pair. Extend hull-specific equipment configuration for
  multiple drives without changing existing monohull behavior. Source confirms
  CPP, not waterjets. Exact Fjordbris shaft/gear arrangement is not established
  here; do not infer shaft count solely from engine count. Start an original
  two-drive layout only with an explicit documented machinery choice.
- ImportedDraftVessel adds SeatSocket interactions, but no passenger manifest,
  terminal boarding flow or fare service was found in the current port/cargo/
  company systems. Old passenger-role/registration labels are not a service.
- HarbourDeploy.terminal_families_for_record recognizes actual fishing/cargo/
  bulk equipment. Add a passenger capability based on usable accommodation and
  boarding equipment, then prefer passenger berths. A helm/crew chair alone must
  not classify today's freighters and trawlers as ferries.
- ShipGangway currently chooses 1 m straight side halfwalls and builds a 0.90 m
  walkway with a 0.60 maximum slope tangent. Preserve it for existing boats.
  Passenger bow boarding needs a wider authored interface and separate, gentler
  operating limits. An existing maximum walking slope is not an accessibility
  design target or certification.
- Port plans are persistent geographic/berth records. Add passenger facilities
  deliberately at selected suitable sites; do not regenerate existing harbours,
  move terrain or silently replace occupied berths. Start with two isolated
  terminals, then make the generated-world integration save-compatible.
- The current company/UI rebuild includes uncommitted and untracked dependencies.
  Preserve those changes and do not sweep them into a ferry commit.

Small complete gameplay slice

Purchase and deploy the ferry through existing shipwright/harbourmaster services.
Choose a passenger sailing at the terminal. Secure the boat; align the correct
boarding door/ramp; open boarding; count passengers onto valid seats; close and
stow; cast off; drive to a second passenger terminal; secure; disembark; settle
one payment. Handle full capacity, wrong destination, obstructed/unsafe ramp,
interrupted boarding, cancellation and scratch save/reload without duplicate
passengers or money. Boarding is disabled while moving or unsecured.

Passenger state belongs in a small persistent service record with sailing ID,
vessel UID, origin, destination, counts/seat allocation and settlement state,
using the existing local authority and company ledger. It is not a disguised
ContainerUnit or an NPC-derived account balance. Each completed boarding/alighting
transition must preserve counts; visual actors only represent that state.
Route demand and a simple point-to-point service come first. A timetable can
follow after travel duration is measured: copying real 13-minute departures into
the accelerated game clock would be misleading. Do not build a scheduling empire
before one round trip works.

Performance and acceptance

Seat positions/capacity are data. Batch identical static seat geometry; activate
nearby interactions and detailed interiors when needed. Use pooled visible
passengers at the terminal and in the cabin, with cheap seated/distant variants;
do not run hundreds of full player IK controllers. Logical occupancy must stay
exact even when actors are culled. Stream terminal detail with the existing port
system and author 3D LOD meshes for its distant silhouette.

Build order for the next implementation batch
1. Measured arrangement plus imported twin-hull physics/collision support in an
   isolated scene. Verify the tunnel is open and old vessels remain unchanged.
2. Reusable Blender exterior/interior and two terminal layouts. Assemble/save/
   reload through the actual shipyard. Inspect bow, broadside, stern, helm,
   passenger aisle and terminal approach in real daytime/night Godot renders.
3. Walk from waiting room across a moving ramp into a seat and up to the helm;
   review alignment under wave motion. No changes to accepted character gait.
4. Add the lean passenger service and one full two-terminal round trip; verify
   counts, seat capacity, handling/braking, payment and scratch persistence.
5. Integrate selected generated ports without changing saved geography, then
   measure close-up and approaching-port frame time. Mark unfinished parts
   honestly; a fast physics test alone is not visual or gameplay acceptance.
