Rock coast and shoals - generation 10

Replaced the uniform near-shore 3 m apron with varied exponential rock faces.
Above-water shelves narrowed from 36-220 m to 5-35 m; steeper rock faces rise
behind them. Historical port-site acceptance is still separate from rendered
terrain; existing port terrain overrides remain in place.

Seeded submerged bedrock formations extend the actual elevation surface around
islands and farther offshore. Narrow elongated crests are 0.7-3.2 m deep, with
steep flanks merging into the basin. Indexed 1 km bins avoid scanning the complete
feature set on every terrain query. Mesh, charts, depth queries and markers all
sample the same surface. These are not decorative rock props or chart-only dots.

Poles target those formations, not arbitrary coastline contours. Sites must be
80 m offshore, 1.5-4 m deep and outside port exclusion zones. The pointer sector
must deepen to 12 m or more within 160 m. Pole streaming remains camera based.
Checks with ports produced 151 / 163 / 144 marks for seeds 42 / 1212599375 / 77127.
The same seeds include 69 / 71 / 72 formations over 1 km offshore and 7 / 7 / 9
shallow crests between opposing shores 1 km apart. 196 of 200 tested coast samples
per seed exceeded 15 m depth 50 m offshore, rather than a blanket shallow apron.

Fixed water-side clipping using inverted endpoint signs against a non-inverted
midpoint, which opened mesh holes. Complementary saddle cells now fill the middle
instead of leaving gaps. Regression checks sum projected triangle area to verify
a complete 1 km terrain chunk and preserve the existing terrain test suite.

Chart zoom now requests detail by actual resolution gain, instead of treating
all 512-pixel world overviews as detailed enough. Detail tiles are 512 pixels.
