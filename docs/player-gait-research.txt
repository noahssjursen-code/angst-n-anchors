Primary sources reviewed

1. Daniel Holden — Inverse Kinematics and Foot Locking (2026)
https://theorangeduck.com/page/inverse-kinematics-foot-locking
Read the technical article. Preserve the character of an incoming pose while
correcting contacts; reconcile root and foot velocity. Allow heel movement
around forefoot support. Excessive pelvis lowering to enforce contact damages
the gait. His athletic toe-contact observation is not a universal rule that
ordinary walking must land toe-first.

2. Rune Skovbo Johansen — Dynamic Walking with Semi-Procedural Animation,
GDC 2009 slides
https://runevision.com/tech/locomotion/Dynamic_Walking_GDC_2009.pdf
Read the slide text (not the complete thesis). A footbase supports heel/toe roll;
phase events distinguish lift, flight and landing. Predict footprints from
linear AND angular motion. Adapt curved swing paths while retaining reference
motion. Grounded feet drive support height more than airborne feet. Independent
leg cycles help start and stop without freezing a foot mid-flight.

3. Adamczyk & Kuo — Redirection of center-of-mass velocity during the
step-to-step transition of human walking (2009)
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19648412/
Read the abstract and accessible article extracts. In ten participants, body
velocity redirection was spread around double support rather than occurring
instantaneously at a contact switch. The reported timing is evidence for a
continuous transfer, not a universal timing constant for our character.

4. Xu, Carlton & Rosengren — Anticipatory postural adjustments for altering
direction during walking (2004)
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15262627/
Read the primary abstract. Eight participants adjusted posture before a turn;
turn angle, pivot foot and speed affected the preparation. This supports looking
ahead when planning a step, rather than correcting crossed legs afterwards.

5. Roche & Torres-Cros (Ubisoft) — Fitting the World: A Biomechanical Approach
to Foot IK, GDC 2016
https://gdcvault.com/play/1023009/Fitting-the-World-A-Biomechanical
Read the session overview only; did not watch the full talk. It identifies
reactive correction as a source of jerky knees and procedural-looking motion,
and describes a predictive approach that preserves animation.

Diagnosis from our code and captures (our assessment, not source claims)

- CharacterLegIK reconstructs from bind and synthesizes the leg, pelvis and arm
  motions separately. Reconstructing from rest avoids numerical drift, but we
  have no complete reference gait whose full-body timing and posture is accepted.
- _update_drop primarily solves a reach constraint. Adding a permanent walking
  crouch can hide reach errors while creating the wrong silhouette.
- _ankle uses a constant offset from a ground point. Heel peel is added later;
  it does not represent the whole boot rolling through contact and swing.
- Foot targets follow linear travel. Turn handling is mostly lane clamps,
  recovery checks and a new body-yaw limit; it does not predict angular travel.
- A single swing arc and carry easing cannot express all of forward, backward,
  lateral and turning foot paths convincingly.
- Existing metrics detect some slips/crossings/length changes, not believable
  weight transfer. They cannot certify art quality.

Next implementation, in order

1. Establish new reference motion on this rig: forward, backward and lateral
   walking, with coherent pelvis, knees, ankles, torso and arm timing. Reference
   curves may be procedural; they still need deliberate full-body poses. Match
   their stride and cadence to actual controller displacement. Review on land.
2. Represent boot heel and forefoot contact explicitly. Roll the boot through
   loading, stance and push-off; retain natural ankle rotation during swing.
   Apply bounded two-bone correction with stable knee direction and soft reach.
   Keep bone lengths fixed. Do not chase a bad target by sinking the whole body.
3. Plan steps through acceleration and yaw. Reversal/stop completes a reachable
   placement with a continuous trajectory; the following placement redirects.
   Separate the immediate input/camera response from visual body reorientation.
4. Blend body support continuously across contact phases. Avoid a square-wave
   weight shift triggered solely by the other foot's swing boolean.
5. Reapply the existing support-frame and wave adaptation to the accepted gait.
   Deck carry and speed modulation are retained, then checked for regressions.

Keep this small: no physics-driven whole-body simulator, motion-matching
framework or unrelated model/controller rewrite is required for this pass.
