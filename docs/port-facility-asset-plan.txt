PORT FACILITIES AND ASSET REQUIREMENTS - 6 October 2026
Research/design record; no runtime layout changes in this pass.

Decision
--------
Generate facilities and working areas from the port's actual services and cargo
handling needs. Import/export is a cargo direction, not a building archetype.
One shared handling area can serve both directions while cargo identities,
owners and inventories remain separate. Do not give every plot a small shed.

What exists in the game
----------------------
PortData: identity, size, region, berth count, maximum ship class, trade_profile,
layout_graph, fuel/fishing/lighthouse/foghorn flags, population and features.
PortTradeProfile: active import/export slots, fixed mature destiny lists, theme.
Ordinary general cargo (internal ID provisions) is added in both directions.
PortFishingService adds fresh_groundfish imports for eligible ports; Expander
then checks actual realized berths before advertising fish landing.
CommodityCatalog: handling mode, terminal family and commodity identity.
PortLayoutGraph.initial_attributes: foundation, berth_plan and land_plan.
Land plan apron pads: stable id, role, commodity_id, zone, footprint, position,
orientation and template. These describe sites, not operational storage capacity.

Problems located
----------------
1. PortApronPadCatalog already defines harbour_office, parking_apron, workshop,
   crew_welfare and chandlery. Only office is required. Parking, workshop,
   welfare, customs and chandlery are absent from active V1 placement lists.
2. Active universal decoration is general_warehouse and fuel_bunker. General
   cargo adds both provisions_depot and provisions_cold. Containers likewise
   add container_yard and container_stack. This can multiply sites for one
   handling function before its required capacity is known.
3. The current imported fallback ignores role and puts warehouse_12x18 on
   every sufficiently large unfilled apron plot, including harbour_office.
   Replace this fallback with role-aware facility recipes.
4. CommodityCatalog currently gives every trade a dedicated quay; general
   cargo and containers share their own import/export group, while other
   groups are commodity-and-direction specific. family_allows_shared_quay
   returns false. Shared storage is possible without first merging berths.
   Actual berth consolidation is a separate change to live harbour logic.
5. Apron placement's berth preference still looks at asphalt_stations, while
   uses_asphalt_dock currently returns false for all commodities. New facility
   connections must resolve actual quay station IDs and access points.
6. A 22 m site lattice and two-cell gap can reserve 44 m between neighbouring
   sites. It cannot serve as the placement grid for doors, cars or loading bays.
   Keep coarse parcels, but design their contents in metres with real clearance.
7. Inland trade decoration is scattered separately from the berth/apron plan.
   It does not establish connected, authoritative industrial premises.
8. Fuel-point flag is currently set true by Expander; do not confuse a vessel
   refuelling service with a port trading bulk diesel/crude/LNG. Verify realized
   service equipment independently, as fish landing already does.

Asset selection rules (proposed, not implemented)
------------------------------------------------
COMMON CORE: one admin/service precinct, connected vehicle access, pedestrian
route, modest staff/visitor parking, lighting, bins, fire/rescue points and
quay fittings. Scale down to a small office/service hut for tiny landings.
Scale by usable site area and working capacity, not by commodity count alone.

HARBOUR OFFICE: distinct one-/two-storey coastal industrial office, public
entrance/canopy, harbour-facing windows, sign, noticeboard, service counter,
staff rooms and equipment cupboard. Use modular walls/corners/windows/doors,
roof, stair/landing and interior pieces. Reception must connect to existing
harbourmaster/shipwright/freight interactions; do not silently move NPCs into
inaccessible solid shells. Small ports can combine office and crew welfare.

PARKING: staff/visitor car bays next to office, separate service-van/loading bays,
and truck/trailer waiting only where cargo traffic warrants it. Reusable kerbs,
endcaps, ramps, wheel stops, markings, signs and gate/barrier. Car stalls, access
aisles, loading bays and truck turning space are separate footprints. Initial
art-layout targets such as 2.6 x 5.2 m car bays are game design values, not a
claim of compliance with any local road/building standard. Keep walking access
to the office outside crane reach and heavy-vehicle turning areas.

GENERAL CARGO (provisions): shared open hardstanding/laydown yard with marked
storage bays and handling aisles; selectively add covered transit shed/canopy
for cargo needing shelter. Assets: pallet/crate bundles, timber dunnage, cable
reels, bundled material, loading doors, rack ends, barriers and signs. Reuse
existing playable CargoYardPad/handling components; decorative stacks must not
duplicate authoritative freight or advertise fake inventory.

CONTAINERS: shared import/export container yard, numbered real 20/40-foot slots,
empty-container area and handling lanes. Reuse existing ISO container models.
Add reefer cabinets only when refrigerated cargo is actually supported; leave
space for handling vehicles before populating stacks. Yard is primarily space
and circulation, not an enclosed warehouse or one building per direction.

ORE / COAL: separate commodity stockpile compartments within a shared bulk
precinct, retaining walls, loading hopper, conveyor supports, transfer housings,
access stairs and weighbridge on larger sites. Shared precinct does not mean
mixing commodities or assuming the current berth controller can merge berths.

GRAIN: enclosed dry storage/silos, elevator tower, covered conveyor and loading
spout. Grain must not inherit the open ore stockpile artwork simply because
both use bulk cargo gameplay.

LIQUID: product-specific tanks, containment walls, pipe racks, valves, pump
house and transfer arms. Diesel/crude may share the site services but retain
separate product systems. LNG needs a visibly different insulated tank/process
kit; terminal_family=liquid is insufficient to select one generic tank model.

FISH LANDING: existing pump/receiving equipment, appropriate shore tanks, wash
area, drained working floor, fish tubs, covered sorting/landing canopy and cold
storage/loading face where appropriate. Use has_fish_landing plus realized
berths. The legacy name provisions_cold does not make all general cargo cold.

SERVICE: workshop/chandlery/welfare when the port supports those functions,
refuel point where realized, shore power/water, waste enclosure. Lighthouse and
foghorn follow existing flags and navigational placement, not every cargo yard.

Data changes needed
-------------------
Add an explicit facility layer inside the existing land_plan; do not create a
parallel economy or derive storage capacity from mesh size. Proposed fields:
id, kind, commodity_ids, served_berth_ids, parcel_polygon, building footprints,
access points, vehicle routes, pedestrian routes, keepouts, phase and recipe
version. Separate required needs from placed facilities and record unmet needs
when a site is too constrained. Mature destiny can reserve expansion space;
only active trade/realized services should populate operational equipment.
Deduplicate compatible handling requirements across import/export lists while
retaining product separation, direction and ownership in existing freight data.
Throughput, dwell time, target occupancy and vehicle mix are not established
facility-sizing inputs today. Until defined, use documented size-tier layouts,
not fictional precise capacity inferred from trade slot count.

Preserve existing saved graphs and IDs. Read old role pads through a compatible
presentation adapter; apply new parcel planning only to newly generated graphs
or an explicit migration. Never erase captain modifications to rebuild scenery.

First application: reviewed Gronnvik, seed 424242
------------------------------------------------
Current reviewed port has general cargo, iron ore export and fish landing;
four apron roles are office, provisions depot, provisions store and general
warehouse. Replace this repeated-shed composition with an office/parking
precinct; one shared general-cargo yard with selective covered storage; an ore
working/storage precinct; and a fish receiving/cold-chain area. Keep its current
three berth IDs while testing facility access. Do not add a container terminal
or LNG plant when those trades are not active. Refuelling is a separate service.

Build order
-----------
1. Role-aware recipes and office/public parking precinct, including usable
   entrance and existing NPC access. Retain saved blueprint priority.
2. Shared open-yard parcels, loading lanes and selective covered storage.
3. Specialist ore/fish areas for this actual port; then other commodity kits.
4. Verify small, constrained and larger port profiles, saved graph reloads,
   walking, vehicle clearances, cargo handling and mooring/crane exclusion zones.
5. Once visual port layout reads correctly, perform Noah's requested semi-realistic
   material pass: worn paint, exposed metal, localized rust, roughness/normal
   detail, damp concrete and patched paving, preserving custom paint controls.
