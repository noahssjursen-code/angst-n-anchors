PERSONAL PORTS: DATA MODEL, BUILDING KIT AND IMPLEMENTATION ORDER
14 October 2026 | Research/design proposal, not an implemented save format

Scope
-----
Reviewed the current game on feature/player-inventory and the sibling
angst-n-anchors-mp service. Commissioned an independent source/capture review.
No gameplay, database, deployed server or existing save was changed in this pass.
The accompanying JSON is a proposed fixture, not something today's loader accepts.
Example: docs/examples/personal-port-snapshot-v1.example.json
It is a coal-port extract: 35 placed pieces, one berth, 25 tonnes in stock,
a 10-tonne buy order, and 12,000 NOK plus receiving space reserved for that order.
Company/account/geography records remain explicit external dependencies. It is
not a complete world backup. JSON syntax, ID references and sample reservation
totals were checked; production schema/engine validation does not yet exist.
All proposed dimensions/capacities/prices below are game design starting points,
not real port engineering standards.

Recommendation
--------------
Define the persistent port records and their rules FIRST. Then implement a small
working kit against those records. Do not create an extensive decorative library
and discover later that none of its equipment has stock, ownership or service data.

Prove two complete loops before factories:
1. Build quay + coal storage + compatible crane, publish a funded coal buy order,
   receive another company's coal, preserve the stock and payment across restart.
2. Build fish receiving + chilled storage, buy landed catch, preserve species,
   quantity/quality and ownership, then sell or collect it from that inventory.

The same port model must serve NPC companies and player companies. Company control
(player/NPC) changes who issues commands, not storage or trading rules. A solo
proprietor can own land directly initially; later transferring ownership to a
company must not move or recreate the port.

WHAT EXISTS TODAY
-----------------
- PlotBuildLayout v3 is a useful construction document: plot, revision, item IDs,
  integer 2 m cells, quarter turns, and saved support depth. It round-trips JSON.
  The showcase holds documents only in RAM. It has no persistent ownership,
  port identity, inventory, company access policy or operational berths.
- CoastalPlotIndex derives 200 x 200 m parcels and world-qualified plot IDs.
  It deliberately does NOT implement ownership. Its geography includes review
  flags for ambiguous/multiple coastline sections; not every green square is
  automatically suitable for private construction.
- Generated ports use PortData + PortLayoutGraph (foundation, berth_plan,
  land_plan/facility_plan). PortFacilityPlan already reasons about commodity
  families, access routes and compatible berths. Do not throw this away.
- WorldGateway exposes send_command, query_projections, projection caches,
  interest retention and event subscriptions. LocalWorldBackend handles part
  of that contract, but its projection dictionaries are transient. Do not assume
  that the existence of query APIs already provides durable local world saves.
- The Go server already has versioned command/result/event/projection envelopes,
  request IDs, expected revisions, commit cursors and PostgreSQL transactions.
  Its existing command_receipts, world_events and world_projections tables are
  reusable infrastructure, not yet a player-port feature.
- The Go gameplay domain contains Company (including members), Cargo, Contract,
  Vessel and Construction records. Some of that domain is exercised in an
  isolated gameplay lab, not yet a complete production company economy.
  GDScript CompanyContracts also embeds inventory_lots, warehouse_leases and
  facility summaries. Reconcile these representations; do not invent a second
  unrelated company or inventory authority.
- Current cargo manifests are explicitly presentation records. They cannot be
  trusted to create saleable inventory or credit a player's account.
- OreMound.take returns the quantity requested without depleting a stock balance.
  The local fish-landing path clears the receiving buffer after settlement.
  Neither behaviour is sufficient for persistent player-owned depot stock.

UNITS AND CONSTRUCTION
----------------------
Current visible kit:
  foundation: 4 x 4 m
  quay / timber pier: 8 x 4 m (4 x 2 internal cells, NOT 4 x 2 metres)
  workshop: 8 x 8 m
  warehouse: 12 x 8 m
  all deck tops: 2 m above the fixed sea datum

Retain the ONE construction plane. Camera height never changes it.
Keep 4 x 4 m as the ordinary structural tile. Add an 8 x 8 brush and rectangular
fill for larger yards, potentially storing the resulting ordinary tiles first.
Keep 8 x 4 quays: useful rectangles are not inherently a mistake. A quay edge,
a platform slab, and a complete berth are different concepts.
Use automatic edge fittings and dedicated sockets for small items, rather than
requiring bollards, ladders, fenders or lights to occupy a whole 4 m tile.

Keep footprint stepping for painting platforms. For buildings, adopt the hovered
foundation corner/edge or a shared 4 m origin: a 12 x 8 warehouse must fit a
12 x 8 foundation patch starting 4 m from the plot origin. Do not force each
building onto an unrelated 12 m lattice. This is a change to propose, not applied.

Separate:
  physical footprint = the space the object occupies
  support footprint = the area that must be built underneath
  working clearance = door access, crane sweep, handling aisle, hose approach
  water clearance = berth length/beam/draft and route into navigable water

The current crane pedestal is 3.6 x 3.6 m before its default 1.5 scale, or
5.4 x 5.4 m: an 8 x 4 slab alone is too narrow. Joined slabs / an 8 x 8 equipment
pad can support the base, but do not represent its whole operational area.
Validate both ends of a crane transfer against its actual reach and access.

Immediate construction gaps:
- Require complete appropriate support below facilities.
- Reject buried buildings/obstructed doors at the fixed plane. Show unsuitable
  terrain clearly; excavation/earthworks can be a separate later feature.
- Block removal of occupied support, or present a deliberate dependent removal.
- Validate ownership across the UNION of adjacent owned plots, not one square
  at a time. Allow a building to cross an internal boundary between owned plots.
- Reserve open water for berth approach/turning. Owning shoreline must not let
  a player silently block a public fairway.
- Bound support depth and construction cost; do not make deep-ocean foundations
  free merely because the renderer can draw long concrete columns.
- Distinguish structural undo from rewinding commerce: on a live server Ctrl+Z
  requests a validated inverse edit. It must not erase stock, completed sales
  or another player's work after dependencies have changed.

MINIMUM PRACTICAL KIT
--------------------
Infrastructure:
  4 x 4 foundation; larger fill brush; quay edge/corner; supported pier;
  usable shore/deck connection; automatic bollard/fender/ladder sockets;
  berth selection and visible depth/approach/clearance; pedestrian access.
  Do not require a full office building just to own a small landing.

Coal / iron ore:
  bounded stockpile bay, retaining walls, suitable grab crane, receiving/loading
  point and safe clear path. Each bay has a finite volume/mass capacity and an
  accepted commodity rule. Coal and ore occupy separate inventory compartments.
  Larger terminals can later add hoppers, conveyors and loaders.
  Pile size/fill derives from stock; individual lumps are not network entities.

Fish:
  landing/receiving point compatible with the current boat catch system,
  weighing/sorting function, chilled storage and a collection/loading point.
  Current pumped catch and future boxed fish need different handling adapters.
  A small depot can package these services in one placeable.
  Water, ice, drainage and power are credible visual/service requirements;
  bespoke pipe/electrical networks are not required for the first playable loop.

Timber / steel:
  open or covered laydown bays; logs, bundled timber, plate stacks and coils
  are different cargo presentations. Iron ORE can be a pile; manufactured steel
  plates should be stacked/bundled, not represented by the ore mound.
  Stocks need commodity IDs, unit/mass/volume and storage rules regardless of art.

Containers / general cargo:
  marked actual-size storage slots, handling space and a compatible lifting
  point. A 20 ft box is about 6.058 x 2.438 m; 40 ft is 12.192 x 2.438 m.
  A 4 x 4 structural module is not a container slot. Reserve separate working
  lanes. Support mixed contents in one container; the container is packaging,
  not a commodity that replaces its contents.

Later:
  grain silos, product-specific liquid tanks/pumps, drying/salting/freezing,
  sawmill, metal processing, shipyard input/output stores, residents' shops.
  Do not implement them all before there is a conserved inventory/transfer loop.
  Recipes consume specified input lots and produce specified output lots.
  Residents/NPC buyers must actually consume purchased goods according to a
  demand rule; a market sign alone must not mint unlimited revenue.

DATA CONTRACT TO ESTABLISH
-------------------------
Use one versioned JSON-compatible contract at both authority backends.
Storage adapters differ; meaning, units and validation do not.

Port:
  stable id, world_id, name, owner {kind,id}, operator_company_id, claimed plot
  IDs, build origin, access policy, layout revision and construction reference.
  The company refers to the port by ID, not an embedded second copy.
  Public discovery shows useful services and offers; internal account/member
  data is private.

PlotClaim:
  world_id + plot_id unique; owner; port_id; claim revision; authority timestamp.
  Claim acquisition and payment are atomic. Check adjacency/ownership and
  navigable access. Geography IDs are derived; deeds are durable world records.

PortConstruction:
  layout ID/revision; pinned module catalogue revision; explicit coordinate and
  deck datum; placements with stable IDs, definition ID/version, local position,
  rotation, finishes, support extent and mounting relationships.
  Use integer millimetres for authored positions/support dimensions. The
  4 m structural grid is an editing policy (4000 mm), not the precision of
  every asset or a reason to resize ISO containers.
  No independently adjustable placement Y for this kit: deck attachment is
  declared, support extends downward, building geometry upward.
  Preserve immutable snapshots/catalogue definitions when replay is promised.
  Unknown definitions must produce an explicit compatibility error, not silently
  vanish as the current prototype importer can do with invalid rows.

Facility:
  id, port_id, placed_instance_ids, definition/version, operator/owner,
  configuration, state, inventory IDs and service bindings.
  A definition supplies allowed capabilities; user configuration can narrow
  them, not grant a tiny shed unlimited capacity or incompatible handling.
  Typed modules: storage, transfer, processing, service, market access.
  A fish depot can have receiving + cold-storage modules; a crane has transfer
  capability and links to a berth and compatible inventories. A coal store
  does not need its own custom economy singleton.

Berth:
  stable id, port_id, edge/pose, length/beam/draft limits, approach/keepout,
  mooring/access sockets and equipment bindings. Runtime occupancy is separate.
  Do not advertise a trade service just because a decorative crane exists.

Inventory and lots:
  inventory_id, physical location/capacity, permitted commodity/condition rules;
  lots with stable IDs, commodity_id, owner, custodian, quantity/unit, condition
  and origin; reservations as separate referenced records.
  Ownership and location are independent: another company's consignment may
  occupy your yard without becoming your property.
  Quantities use canonical integer base units: grams, millilitres or pieces.
  Catalogued units cannot be mixed. Display kg/tonnes/litres as appropriate.
  Capacity has independent mass and volume limits, and slot limits when relevant.
  Keep fish species/condition/traceability through transfers; preserve individual
  catch records where needed for the library, aggregate compatible lots for trade.
  Existing float kg/tonne/unit records require explicit adapters and migration.

Packaging / cargo:
  extend the existing Cargo/ContainerUnit/BulkCargoLot/CargoConsignment contracts.
  A container holds lot references and quantities, plus its own dimensions,
  colour, carrier, slot location and delivery references.
  A pile/tank/crate is a visual/storage form, not an independent copy of stock.
  One physical quantity has one custody location: ship, crane payload, quay slot
  or storage. A transfer-in-progress must never duplicate both ends.

MarketOrder:
  buy/sell, commodity and quality filters, quantity/price basis, owner, port and
  inventory IDs, delivery/pickup terms, expiry/status and resource reservations.
  A buy order reserves money and receiving capacity; a sell order reserves stock.
  Accepting a delivery releases neither until success/cancel/expiry rules apply.
  Storage and a crane make receiving possible. An explicit funded BUY ORDER makes
  "sell coal here" available. A sell listing and freight contract are separate:
  changing owner does not teleport cargo to the buyer.

Money:
  use NOK explicitly. The current canonical Go company uses integer balance_nok.
  Keep integer NOK settlement initially, with an explicit quantity price basis
  and documented rounding at settlement; do not invent ambiguous new marks fields.
  Existing legacy *_marks/value_gold fields remain compatibility adapters only.
  Fractional pricing can later introduce a versioned minor-unit money contract,
  but must not silently reinterpret any existing balance.

ProcessingJob (later, but leave the reference seam):
  facility/recipe version, input reservations, output inventory, start/due world
  tick and status. Reserve inputs and output space. Completion must be idempotent.
  Persist work/timestamps, not frame-by-frame machinery or physics.

SAVE, FETCH AND SUBSCRIBE
------------------------
Local:
  Store durable WORLD state separately from a captain's personal loadout.
  A versioned world snapshot contains records + applied event cursor + pinned
  world/catalogue identity. Durable command receipts survive restart.
  Use an atomic snapshot replacement with backup and/or a transactional local
  store; append-only committed events allow replay since a checkpoint.
  Extending LocalWorldBackend with durable world storage is real work still
  required. Its current in-memory projection cache is not that implementation.

Server:
  Extend the existing Go authority handlers and PostgreSQL transaction.
  Reuse world_events, command_receipts and world_projections for ordered changes.
  Add queryable port/claim/facility/inventory/order records or transactional
  indexed projections where required. Use a database uniqueness constraint for
  (world_id,plot_id), not only a client check. The current generic projection
  key is (kind,entity_id): IDs must be world-qualified, or migrate the key.
  State rows, money/stock/capacity reservations, receipt and emitted events must
  commit together. Cache mutation and publication happen after success.
  Don't post a client-authored full inventory or bank balance as trusted truth.

Commands through WorldGateway (proposed names, not existing endpoints):
  property.claim, port.create, port.build.apply, port.facility.configure,
  market.order.place/cancel, cargo.transfer.request/complete.
  Every request carries request_id and the relevant expected revision.
  Authentication supplies actor; server checks company permissions, definitions,
  physical feasibility and resource availability. Never trust owner fields.

Queries / subscriptions:
  Reuse /v2/commands, /v2/projections, /v2/events and /v2/state transport.
  Add bounded/paginated port discovery and market queries; these are not complete
  today. Keep remote market queries independent of visual render range.
  Nearby clients retain a port interest, receive a snapshot at cursor C, then
  apply committed events after C. Confirm interest establishment and close the
  fetch/subscribe race using the existing cursor/replay mechanism.
  Gaps trigger resync; stale/duplicate events are ignored by revision.
  Public port/layout/service/visible-stock projections are separate from private
  company lots, money, reservations and procurement terms.
  Unloading a port's scene removes meshes and subscriptions, not world stock,
  buildings, claims or processing jobs. The authority keeps operating.
  Start with bounded whole-port snapshots. Add spatial layout chunks and cached
  immutable construction hashes when measured size requires it; do not send all
  ports' detailed inventories to every client.

Reconstruction:
  Snapshot + catalogue/version + world identity + subsequent committed events
  rebuild data and then presentation. No saved physics is required.
  Exact historical replay additionally requires retaining checkpoints/events and
  old definitions; simply storing only the latest JSON cannot recreate yesterday.
  Live construction changes need derived terrain/berth/navigation/chart/LOD cache
  invalidation. Generated NPC ports must seed once, then evolve through the same
  persistent records instead of regenerating over player edits.

HANDLING THE EXISTING PORT SYSTEM
--------------------------------
Keep PortLayoutGraph as the construction/geometry seam; evolve it with an explicit
player-built layout variant or adapter for PlotBuildLayout. Do not force a player's
arbitrary slab layout into initial generated berth_plan assumptions.
Add a common Port aggregate that references geometry, facilities, berths and stock.
Generated NPC ports become seed producers for those records. PortData/chart views
read projections. Retire overlapping legacy facility/inventory copies only through
explicit migration, rather than bolting a new economy alongside them.
Keep broad NPC starter services initially so new worlds remain playable. Let player
ports compete on capacity, location, price and processing; do not slash NPC ports
before the replacement economy is functional.

IMPLEMENTATION ORDER AND ACCEPTANCE
-----------------------------------
1. Versioned records, catalogue definitions, identity/units, validators and sample
   fixtures. Choose the first coal and fish service definitions.
2. Snapshot/load/restart through local world storage and Go/PostgreSQL.
   Run identical rule fixtures in Godot and Go; do not assume code sharing.
3. Connect the builder to authoritative build commands, claims and construction
   projections. Add support, access/terrain and berth-clearance validation.
4. Finite coal/fish inventories, commodity-compatible transfer, funded buy orders,
   other-company delivery, settlement, collection and restart.
5. Production recipes and public sell orders; later resident/NPC demand,
   shipyard bills of materials, utilities and specialised terminal types.

Required data-only scenarios:
- Rebuild identical placement IDs, support depths and contents after restart.
- Repeated request ID charges/builds/transfers exactly once.
- Two players edit the same revision: one rejects/rebases cleanly.
- Two companies attempt the same claim or last stock: exactly one succeeds.
- Reject unsupported/blocked/overlapping/outside-owned-land construction.
- Full depot, wrong commodity, inaccessible berth or out-of-reach crane rejects.
- Partial unload conserves cargo across ship / suspended payload / depot.
- Interrupted job resumes or rolls back according to persisted state.
- Cancel/expiry releases only its own reservations, with no vanished goods.
- Rejoining/out-of-range clients reconstruct new and deleted pieces correctly.
- Company access rules protect stock and funds while publishing allowed offers.
- Undo cannot remove a busy crane or a warehouse containing/reserving goods.
- Missing catalogue version or wrong world checksum fails explicitly.
- When processing arrives: offline elapsed time uses saved world rules, outputs
  are produced once, and paused single-player does not silently gain MP elapsed time.

INDEPENDENT REVIEW
------------------
A separate agent received a fresh context and reviewed source plus the archived
native capture; it did not run a second interactive playtest or change files.
It independently recommended:
  keep 4 x 4 m foundations and the single sea+2 m plane;
  fix support/terrain/deletion rules before adding many assets;
  anchor large buildings to actual foundation patches;
  treat the 8 x 4 m quay as a component rather than a berth;
  reuse existing handling presentation but replace infinite/cleared stock with
  finite owned depot inventories;
  defer factories, conveyor networks and a mandatory large harbour office.

REAL-WORLD RESEARCH AND GAME INTERPRETATION
-------------------------------------------
FAO identifies fish landing infrastructure together with ice, clean water,
electricity and efficient handling/storage as important parts of the supply chain.
Game inference: a fish depot represents receiving plus preservation, not just
a crate and a sales trigger.
https://www.fao.org/flw-in-fish-value-chains/multidimensional-approach/services-and-infrastructure/en
https://www.fao.org/4/T0388E/T0388E03.htm

Port of Rotterdam describes distinct dry-bulk products with terminal handling,
storage and onward transport. Game inference: stock compartments and handling
compatibility matter more than one generic infinite resource heap.
https://www.portofrotterdam.com/en/logistics/cargo/dry-bulk

Euroports' own terminal description lists steel plates/coils/beams and forest
products with dedicated equipment, warehousing and cargo-specific handling.
Game inference: separate ore piles, manufactured metal bundles and timber stores.
Do not copy its huge terminal scale into a starter player port.
https://www.euroports.com/terminal/break-bulk-terminal-ta1207-antwerp-belgium/

Carrier specifications provide real container dimensions; keep those independent
of the construction tile size. Internal game ContainerUnit already models the
20/40 ft external dimensions.
https://www.rclgroup.com/ContainerSpecification
https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/en/services-information/cargo-fleet/container.html
