# COMPONENTS.md — the working-vessel parts catalogue

A reference dataset of **every object that plausibly exists on a working vessel**, written as a
**kit**, not a model set.

Owner direction, 2026-08-09: *"do not tailor these boats, make parts that people can build custom
ships from."* So every row here is the **general form** of a thing — "cleat, 0.15–0.45 m, scales
with line size" — never "the cleat off that Norwegian trawler". If a row only makes sense on one
vessel type, it is the wrong row, and where a part genuinely is type-specific the `notes` column
says so out loud.

This file is **research and vocabulary**. It is not a schema, and nothing here is implemented. It
exists so that whoever writes the `items[]` catalog is choosing from a real list instead of
remembering five fittings.

---

## THE SCALE RULE — read this before you use any number below

**One deck-grid cell is 0.5 m of real world. Two cells per metre.**

- A hull whose grid is **28 × 10 cells is a 14 m × 5 m vessel.** The catalog display strings
  ("14.0 × 5.0 m" for `hull_28x10`) are the **true physical size**; the 28 and 10 are **cell
  counts, not metres**. A 1 m grid was judged too coarse to build detail on, so the cell is half
  a metre.
- **The player is 1.8 m tall** (`scenes/shared/player.tscn`, capsule 1.8, eye 1.6) — that is
  **3.6 cells**. Everything a human touches is sized against that figure.
- `hull_28x10` → **14 × 5 m**: an inshore trawler, which is exactly what R2 is.
  `hull_45x16_cat` → **22.5 × 8 m**: a small fast catamaran, which is exactly what R1 and R3 are.
  Under this convention the reference vessels and the hull catalog finally agree.

> **Known discrepancy, deliberately ignored here.** `WorldUnits.DECK_CELL_M` is currently `1.0`,
> so the world is presently *drawn* at 2× physical scale relative to the 1.8 m player. That is a
> real bug being fixed separately. **This dataset is authored to the intended model, not to the
> current code.** If a number here disagrees with what the running game draws, the running game is
> the thing that is wrong.

### How dimensions are written

Every dimension is given twice:

> **1.0–1.2 m** *(2.0–2.4 cells)*

The metre figure is the real-world size against a 1.8 m person. The cell figure is the same number
at 2 cells/m, so a builder can see immediately whether a part fits the grid.

### Sub-cell parts — the load-bearing distinction

Anything smaller than **0.5 m in its governing dimension cannot be placed on the grid at all.**
Cleats, fairleads, chocks, light fixtures, blocks, sheaves, handrail stanchions, portlights, most
hardware — these are **sub-cell** and need **free positioning with float coordinates**, not cell
snapping. Every such row is flagged **`SUB-CELL`** at the head of its `notes`.

This is not a detail. It decides which half of the parts catalog needs an off-grid placement model
and which half can snap, and it is a large fraction of the catalog — roughly every fitting a human
operates with their hands.

### The failure mode this section exists to prevent

`probe_trawler_bow_bulwark`'s wheelhouse was sized against the wrong reading of the hull scale,
came out at ~10% of LOA where a real trawler is nearly double that, and **every number the gate
records agreed with every other number and they were all wrong together.** A human looking at a
picture caught it. Numbers that agree with each other are not evidence.

## How to read the table

| Column | What it means |
|---|---|
| **object** | Correct nautical name. Common synonyms in parentheses — builders will search for those. |
| **category** | Which group the row belongs to (repeated per row so rows survive being copied out of context). |
| **why** | What it is FOR, concretely. Not "safety" — *"stops a person going over the side when the deck is wet."* A player placing this should understand why they are placing it. |
| **physical** | Form, material, typical colour, how it attaches, one piece or assembly. |
| **dimensions_units** | **Real metres against the 1.8 m figure, then the same in grid cells at 2 cells/m.** A realistic RANGE where vessels differ, plus what the dimension scales with (beam? deck length? nothing?). Where a real-world figure is uncertain it says so rather than inventing precision. |
| **primitive** | Which of the five primitives from `REFERENCES.md` builds it — **spar**, **railing run**, **wire**, **sloped plate**, **sheer band** — or **box/room/deck** if today's primitives already cover it, or **new** with a note on what is missing. |
| **live_or_baked** | **baked** = merges into the static vessel skin. **live** = must stay an interactive node. Rule of thumb: anything the player operates, anything that emits light, anything that moves, anything gameplay counts by identity, and anything a rope or physics body attaches to must stay live. Everything else bakes. |
| **compliance** | If it maps to something `resources/data/vessels/registrations/catalog.json` counts. The vocabulary that file and `scripts/ship/vessel_compliance.gd` actually understand today is: tags `mooring`, `nav_white`, `bulk_hold`, `crane`, `fishing`, `tow`, `cargo`; brick ids `light_nav_port`, `light_nav_stbd`, `light_nav_white`, `light_mast_white`; slots `helm`, `fishing`, `crane`, `mooring`; metrics `exposed_deck_cells`, `doors`, `cargo_cells`, `passenger_capacity`, `equipment_rating`; capability `has_cabin`. Most rows are honestly "—". |
| **notes** | **`SUB-CELL`** flag where applicable, then counts per vessel, usual placement, what it must not collide with, how it varies by vessel type. |

**A note on the primitive column.** Where a row says "new", that is a finding, not a complaint —
the last section of this file collects them. A row that can be assembled from two or three existing
primitives is marked with the assembly, e.g. `spar ×2 + wire` for an A-frame, because that is
exactly the kit-versus-model-set distinction: the catalog entry saves the player the assembly, it
does not do something the player could not do by hand.

**Vessel size assumed throughout.** Unless a row says otherwise, "a working vessel" here means
**8–30 m LOA** — the range the hull catalog covers under the 0.5 m cell convention. Rows that only
make sense above or below that range say so.

## 1. Hull structure — shell, framing, and the lines below the deck edge

Most of this is invisible on a finished vessel, but the *lines* it produces (chine, knuckle,
sheer, rubbing strake) are the entire reason a real hull reads as fast, heavy, or workmanlike and
our extruded box does not.

## 2. Deck, deck edge, and the enclosure above it

The deck edge is where the vessel's identity lives. Bulwark versus guardrail is the biggest
"what kind of boat is this" decision a builder makes, and the cap rail carries the sheer that
`REFERENCES.md` proved the hull loft cannot.

| object | category | why | physical | dimensions_units | primitive | live_or_baked | compliance | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deck plating** (deck, weather deck) | Structure | The surface people and cargo stand on, and the top of the watertight envelope. | Stiffened plate, cambered so water runs to the sides. Non-slip coated; painted in work areas, sometimes bare in cargo areas. | Thickness **5–10 mm**; camber 1/50 of beam → **0.06–0.16 m** *(0.12–0.32 cells)* rise at the centreline. Camber is subtle but its absence is why flat CG decks look like floors. |
| **Deck beam** | Structure | Carries the deck plating across the ship between frames. | Bulb flat or T-section under the deck at each frame, bracketed to the frames at each end. | Depth **80–200 mm** *(0.16–0.4 cells)*, at frame spacing **0.5–0.6 m** *(1–1.2 cells)*. | sloped plate strip | baked | — | **SUB-CELL** in depth. ## 3. Joinery, coatings and markings

Almost none of this is geometry. It is nearly all **surface**, which makes it the cheapest
identity in the file once colour lives in vertex data — `REFERENCES.md` records that two of the
three reference vessels get most of their identity from paint, not from shape.

| object | category | why | physical | dimensions_units | primitive | live_or_baked | compliance | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Butt weld seam** | Joinery | Where two plates meet edge to edge. | **Fillet weld** | Joinery | Where a plate meets another at an angle — every stiffener, bracket, coaming and bulwark stay. Every dimension in this table is set by the human body and **none of it scales with the vessel**.
A 10 m boat and a 200 m ship have the same stair riser, the same door height, the same rung pitch.
